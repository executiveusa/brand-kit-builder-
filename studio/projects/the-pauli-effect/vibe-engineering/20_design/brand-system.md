# Vibe Engineering — Brand System v1

## 1. Brand in one line
**Vibe Engineering teaches people how to direct AI with better thinking.**

## 2. Motto
# VERIFY IT BEFORE EVERYTHING.

This is not a slogan added after the fact. It is the governing behavior of the brand.

## 3. Visual personality
**Urban, editorial, precise, human.**

Urban means real people, real spaces, real culture and real texture—not an effects pack.

Editorial means composition has hierarchy and point of view.

Precise means every mark, frame, line, transition and color has a job.

Human means taste and decisions are visible; AI does not erase the person.

## 4. Color language
- **Ink** — the canvas. Calm, serious, lets imagery and proof signals carry energy.
- **Warm Paper** — human/editorial contrast. Avoid sterile pure white.
- **Proof Acid** — the signature. Selected. Verified. Go forward.
- **Paper Dim** — supporting information only.
- **Reject Red** — critique, error, crossed-out option, needs review. Never decoration.

### Color law
Proof Acid and Reject Red are verbs, not decoration.

## 5. Typography
### Display — Aalto
Use for major statements, title cards, chapter numbers, and the VIBE wordmark exploration. Keep phrases short. Let scale do the work.

### Body — Projekt Blackbird
Use for navigation, teaching copy, captions, interface text, proof notes and supporting information.

### Type behavior
- Maximum two families.
- No fake graffiti fonts to signal youth or urban culture.
- No rasterized type when live type can do the job.
- No mixing five weights/styles to manufacture energy.
- Large type gets room to breathe; body copy stays narrow and easy to scan.

## 6. Imagery
Show **people deciding and making**, not people posing next to technology.

Preferred moments:
- comparing two directions,
- choosing a typeface from the real VIBE kit,
- rearranging a system,
- crossing out a weak option,
- testing on a phone,
- showing work to another person,
- a real business/community/creative outcome being used.

### Image look
- documentary/editorial,
- natural or motivated practical light,
- authentic urban environments,
- tactile close-ups of hands, screens, paper, walls, tools,
- diverse ages and backgrounds without tokenized casting,
- composition with negative space for typography.

### Avoid
- cyberpunk neon city,
- floating holograms,
- fake AI interfaces with illegible text,
- generic hoodies-in-dark-room coding imagery,
- distressed overlays used to manufacture authenticity.

## 7. Graphic language
### The Proof Frame
Thin system lines frame the decision currently being considered.

### The Reject Mark
A hand-drawn-feeling but clean red circle-slash/strike appears only when a choice is rejected. It is intentionally simple—not a graffiti texture.

### The Sequence
When explaining a process, show one next move at a time. The audience should never have to decode the whole system at once.

## 8. Public copy
Short before long.

Preferred:
- Verify It Before Everything.
- You do not need to know code.
- Bring the idea.
- Use your taste.
- See how the pieces connect.
- Check the work.
- Make it yours.

Avoid:
- “revolutionary AI ecosystem,”
- “supercharge your workflow,”
- “unlock limitless potential,”
- long architecture explanations on the main path.

## 9. Brand architecture
**The Pauli Effect** → parent maker/studio.

**Pauli Suite** → family of owned products.

**Vibe Engineering** → method, learning system, and product for AI-era thinking/building discipline.

Public footer lockup:
`Built by The Pauli Effect — 2026 · Part of the Pauli Suite`

## 10. Anti-slop test
Before anything ships, ask:
1. Could this belong to any AI startup? If yes, reject it.
2. Is an effect doing the job that an idea should do? If yes, remove it.
3. Are we using “urban” as a costume? If yes, start over.
4. Does the viewer know what to look at first? If no, simplify.
5. Can every signal be explained? If no, delete it.
