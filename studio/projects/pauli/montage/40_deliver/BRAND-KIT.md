# Montage — Brand Kit v1.0

## 1. Brand core
**Name:** Montage  
**Pronunciation:** /mänˈtäZH/  
**Governing idea:** **Many moments. One story.**

**Definition**  
A montage is an artistic technique or process of combining multiple separate images, video clips, or pieces of media into a single, unified composition.

**Product promise**  
Turn raw clips into a finished montage without becoming an editor.

**Primary action**  
Start a Montage.

## 2. Position
Montage is an AI-assisted video storytelling workspace that starts with intent and source footage—not an empty professional timeline. It helps people find the strongest moments, shape a compressed story, review the cut and export it in the format they need.

## 3. Identity
The primary mark is a framed M drawn as one continuous editorial sequence. It represents both the video frame and the act of separate moments resolving into one composition.

## 4. Typography
**Display:** Instrument Serif  
Use for the name, definition, high-level statements, editorial section openings and cinematic end cards.

**Interface:** Inter  
Use for navigation, controls, metadata, forms, captions and operational surfaces.

**Fallbacks:** Georgia for display; Arial/system sans for interface.

## 5. Color
| Token | Hex | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Ink | `#0A0A0A` | Primary text, mark, dark surfaces |
| Paper | `#F4F1EA` | Primary warm background |
| White | `#FFFFFF` | Reverse mark, contrast surfaces |
| Slate | `#747474` | Secondary text |
| Line | `#D9D5CC` | Rules, dividers, quiet boundaries |
| Signal | `#D7A95B` | Controlled emphasis and timing cue |

Signal gold is not a decorative luxury color. Use it to indicate the important moment, active sequence, selected edit, or CTA accent.

## 6. Layout grammar
- Use whitespace as pacing.
- Let one dominant idea lead each surface.
- Build compositions from purposeful frames, crops and sequences.
- Use hard edges and editorial rules more often than rounded card walls.
- Allow imagery to behave like edited footage: juxtaposition, rhythm, scale shift, crop and sequence.
- Never use random collage merely to make a page look cinematic.

## 7. Motion grammar
Default behavior: **fragment → rhythm → alignment → hold**.

Hard cuts are the primary editorial transition. Dissolves exist only when emotion or temporal change justifies them.

Logo sting: 600–900 ms.  
UI feedback: 80–140 ms.  
Major editorial transition: 220–420 ms.  
Reduced motion: final state immediately.

## 8. Image direction
Prioritize footage that feels observed rather than staged:
- documentary;
- people in motion;
- travel and place;
- making/working;
- events and performance;
- transformation;
- process;
- quiet human detail;
- before/after contrast.

The hero should feel like a real montage: multiple related moments arranged over time or space, not one “AI creator” stock image.

## 9. Voice
Editorial clarity with cinematic confidence.

Use concrete language. Say what the system does. Avoid generic AI claims.

### Core lines
- **Many moments. One story.**
- **Turn raw clips into a finished montage without becoming an editor.**
- **Find the strongest moments.**
- **Build a 30-second story.**
- **Your originals stay untouched.**

### Avoid
- AI magic;
- revolutionary;
- game-changing;
- next-gen creativity;
- feature-count headlines;
- technical architecture in primary marketing copy.

## 10. Product workflow
1. **Bring in your clips** — Google Drive, OneDrive, local footage, screen recordings, photos and audio.
2. **Find the best moments** — metadata, transcript, scene understanding and search.
3. **Build the montage** — selects, sequence, captions, reframing, sound and review cut.
4. **Export anywhere** — 9:16, 16:9, 1:1, highlights, walkthroughs and platform-ready derivatives.

## 11. Hero system
The launch hero should be full-bleed but breathable.

Required content order:
1. logo/nav;
2. large `Montage` display name;
3. pronunciation `/mänˈtäZH/`;
4. exact definition;
5. product promise;
6. `Start a Montage` primary CTA;
7. `Watch Demo` secondary CTA;
8. visible montage sequence showing several different clips becoming one story;
9. small workflow/output cues only after the main idea is understood.

## 12. Social system
### 9:16 reel cover
- top: mark or lockup;
- center: one dominant still or a 2–4 frame montage;
- bottom: concise title, never more than two short lines;
- optional signal rule for series/category.

### 1:1 / 4:5 posts
Use generous paper/ink layouts or photographic crops with one editorial statement. Avoid templated feature-card grids.

### End card
Mark + `Many moments. One story.` + optional URL/CTA. Keep to 1.5–2.5 seconds unless the content requires longer.

## 13. Watermark
Use the mark only. White over dark content, Ink over light content. Place within safe title bounds. Do not animate repeatedly during the content.

## 14. UI behavior
The marketing identity and product UI should feel related but not identical. Operational screens prioritize clarity, trust and speed. Brand typography appears sparingly in the application shell; Inter/system UI carries most controls.

## 15. Accessibility
- Maintain WCAG-compliant contrast for text and controls.
- Do not place essential text directly over ungraded moving footage without a stable contrast field.
- Preserve keyboard focus states.
- Respect reduced motion and reduced transparency.
- Minimum practical touch target: 44×44 CSS px.

## 16. Brand non-negotiables
- Source masters are never visually represented as disposable or destructive-editable.
- The product does not pretend to be “one click magic”; review is part of trust.
- Montage is about compression and coherence, not effects for their own sake.
- The identity should show editing behavior, not generic AI symbolism.

## 17. Launch line
**Montage — Many moments. One story.**
